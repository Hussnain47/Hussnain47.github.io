Deleting trillions of rows. Protecting the system around them.

A two-week Cassandra cleanup using Apache Spark, with Datadog monitoring and a real overload incident to learn from.

ContextData engineering · Khoros Marketing
My roleMigration execution and incident response
TechnologyApache Spark / Cassandra / Datadog
Spark→Cassandra→Monitor

Conceptual overview · not a production topology

The problem

Twitter-related data in Khoros Marketing required removal at a scale of trillions of Cassandra rows. The work was a large-scale deletion, rather than a move to a new data store. Parallel execution had to be balanced against the stability of the Cassandra system.

My contribution

I used Apache Spark to parallelize the deletion and set up Datadog monitoring to track system stability. The migration progressed gradually over two weeks so that its load could be managed over time.

The central tradeoff

Parallelism made the deletion tractable, but it also increased pressure on the serving system. The practical question was how much deletion work Cassandra could absorb while the rollout continued. Monitoring was part of executing the migration, not just a final check.

The overload incident

The rollout included one major Cassandra downtime incident caused by overload. It was handled swiftly, and no data was lost. That incident belongs in the account of the work: the migration was not a zero-downtime operation.

Outcome and limits

The work removed trillions of Twitter-related rows over a two-week rollout. Datadog provided visibility into system stability during execution. The rollout included an overload-related Cassandra outage; the incident was handled swiftly with no data loss.

What I learned

A distributed job can be healthy while its destination is overloaded. Successful bulk operations require attention to the shared system’s limits, and the outcome should include the recovery story as well as the completed work.
